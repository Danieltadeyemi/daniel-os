# Style Guide
*The voice behind the content machine. Read this before drafting anything.*

---

## Who This Is For

Content for a Growth/Marketing expert who brings his whole self — professional expertise, family life, relationships, and worldview — to everything he creates. The core is growth and marketing. The texture is a full human being.

Audience: operators, founders, marketers, and professionals building things in Africa and beyond. People who are serious about their work without taking themselves too seriously.

---

## Voice and Tone

**What it sounds like:**
- Thoughtful, not loud
- Direct, not blunt
- Warm, not sentimental
- Occasionally funny — dry humor, not performative
- Confident in positions, open about uncertainty

**What it never sounds like:**
- Hustle-culture energy
- Sensationalism or manufactured urgency
- Corporate polish or marketing-speak
- Performative humility or false modesty
- Preachy or lecturing

**The test:** Would this feel out of place in a good conversation with a smart friend? If yes, rewrite it.

---

## Core Beliefs (the worldview behind the content)

1. **Growth is systematic, not tactical.** It has components, takes time, and can't be shortcut.
2. **Marketing builds on a working product.** If the product doesn't serve customers, marketing can't rescue it. Marketing's real job is listening to customers and feeding that back to product.
3. **Thinking end-to-end is underrated.** Most failures aren't channel failures — they're execution and follow-through failures.
4. **Most urgencies are overrated.** Taking time brings better clarity.
5. **The world is a museum of people's passion projects.** Everything that exists was made by someone who cared enough to make it.
6. **Rest is discipline, not weakness.** Treat yourself like an athlete — pace matters, off-seasons are real, and the body gives signals worth listening to.
7. **Modeling is leadership.** How you live is the message, especially to those watching closely.

---

## Content Pillars

1. **Growth & Marketing** — the core. Frameworks, failures, contrarian takes, real decisions.
2. **Business Insights** — patterns from operators, conversations, and observations about how things actually work.
3. **People & Relationships** — what conversations and relationships have taught him. Specific people, specific moments.
4. **Family & Life** — being a husband and father, and what that teaches about work, leadership, and presence.
5. **Worldview** — how he sees things. Slow thinking, profound over sensational, the long game.

---

## Structural Rules

- **Lead with the specific, not the general.** Open with a moment, a number, a name — not a thesis.
- **One idea per post.** Don't try to say three things. Say one thing well.
- **Earn the insight.** Don't open with the lesson. Let it land at the end.
- **Short sentences under pressure.** When making a strong point, shorten the sentence.
- **No listicles that are just repackaged obvious advice.** Every list item must say something that requires knowing something.

---

## What to Never Do

- Open with "I" (on LinkedIn especially)
- Use words like: leverage, synergy, unlock, game-changer, revolutionize, crush it, skyrocket
- Moralize or turn a personal story into a lecture
- Pad content to look longer than it is
- Use engagement bait ("Comment YES if you agree")
- Attribute vague wisdom to unnamed "mentors" — name the person or drop the reference
- Manufacture urgency — if something isn't urgent, don't pretend it is

---

## Formats in Use

- **LinkedIn posts** — 150–400 words. Personal stories with a professional insight embedded. No hashtag spam.
- **X/Twitter** — Sharp observations, one-liners with depth, short threads for ideas that need 3–5 beats.
- **Newsletter (Questing)** — Long-form. Interview-driven. Amplifying Africa's best operators.
- **Threads** — Used when one idea has natural sequential logic. Each tweet must stand alone.

---

## The Golden Rule

Write like you're texting a smart friend something you genuinely think they should know. Not performing. Not teaching. Just sharing.
