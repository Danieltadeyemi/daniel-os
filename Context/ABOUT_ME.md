## Company: Startbutton

### What It Actually Does

Startbutton is a Merchant of Record (MoR) layer for Africa. It enables companies to sell into or across African markets without setting up legal entities, payments infrastructure, or compliance stacks in each country.

The problem it solves is coordination failure at scale:
- Fragmented payment systems across 54 countries
- Regulatory complexity that varies by market
- Entity setup requirements and tax overhead
- Compliance that must be managed continuously

Most companies underestimate this until they try to execute. The pain isn't that any one thing is insurmountably hard — it's that payments, regulation, tax, and local nuance all interact at once. That's where expansion breaks.

**What Startbutton does in practice:** Acts as the merchant of record. Handles payments, compliance, and regulatory requirements. Lets companies transact commercially in markets they aren't physically set up in.

### Core Products

**Startbutton Transact** — Collections and payouts across 16 African currencies. Mobile money, bank transfers, cards, USSD, and USD collections. Single API.

**Startbutton Comply** — Tax compliance, regulatory adherence, fraud management, and chargeback handling as the merchant of record. No local legal team required.

**Startbutton Treasury** — FX rate endpoints for Pan-African currencies, wallet and account issuance across multiple countries, stablecoin on/off ramp, cross-border settlement. Priced at $500/issued account/country/month.

**Startbutton Dispense** — Instant local-currency payouts to businesses and partners via single API across all 16 markets.

These are not a funnel — they're a complementary infrastructure suite. A customer can enter at any product and deepen their relationship across the stack.

### Where We Are

Scaling, but still early.

**What's real:**
- Live product with paying customers
- ~$17M monthly processed volume
- Operating in 16 African countries

**What's honest:**
- Positioning is still being refined
- Systems and execution are not yet fully mature
- There's a gap between what Startbutton could be with sharp positioning and what it currently operates like

We're past early traction. Not yet category-defined. The ceiling is much higher than the current execution.

## Market and Competitive Landscape

### The Real Buyer

Typically a **founder, COO, or Head of Expansion/Growth** at a company entering or scaling across African markets.

**Their pressures:**
- Show growth in new markets, fast
- Execute without creating compliance or operational risk
- Prove that Africa is a viable revenue market to internal stakeholders
- Do this without heavy upfront investment or large local teams

They're not just buying payments infrastructure — they're buying a credible answer to the question: *can we actually operate here without blowing up?*

### Why Customers Choose Us — and Why They Don't

**Why they choose Startbutton:**
- Speed: faster market entry vs. entity setup
- Simplicity: one partner vs. multiple local vendors
- Risk reduction: compliance is handled, not managed piecemeal

**Honest layer:** Many don't fully understand the MoR model at first. Part of every sales motion is education and trust-building.

**Why some don't:**
- Choose to build internally (entities + local infrastructure)
- Work with local payment providers who only solve part of the stack
- Don't feel the pain acutely enough yet
- Cost sensitivity against unclear perceived value
- Trust barrier — infrastructure-level decisions require confidence in a partner

### Competitive Landscape

**Biggest competitor: the status quo**
Delaying expansion. Doing nothing. Continuing to operate in limited markets.

**Internal solutions:**
Companies that set up entities and build their own compliance + payments stack. High effort, but seen as "control."

**Local payment providers:**
Flutterwave, Fincra, and others. Solve payments, not the full stack. Adjacent and sometimes referred to as competitors, but they don't hold MoR.

**Adjacent infrastructure players:**
Payments, payroll, compliance vendors who each solve a piece but don't offer a unified layer.

**Direct category competitors:**
dLocal, Airwallex. Competing on coverage, pricing, and ease of use.

## Strategic Themes

### The Three Big Bets

**1. Scale to $100M monthly TPV**
From ~$17M today. Both a demand gen bet (can we consistently drive pipeline?) and a product/retention bet (do customers transact more over time?). This is the near-term commercial pressure everything else serves.

**2. Expand geographic coverage to 20+ countries**
The thesis: coverage matters in this market. Customers expanding across Africa want one partner, not multiple local solutions. Greater footprint strengthens both the value proposition and defensibility.

**3. Become the default fiat ↔ stablecoin infrastructure layer in Africa**
The bet: stablecoins will become a meaningful part of cross-border transactions across Africa's 54 countries. Companies will need a reliable bridge between rails. Startbutton's position is the single integration layer that abstracts away complexity across markets and rails.

This is simultaneously a product build and a narrative ownership play — move early, define the category.

### The Core Tension

**Speed vs. clarity and consistency.**

We need to move fast to hit short-term goals. We're still refining positioning and building systems. These pull in opposite directions constantly — quick execution vs. work that compounds.

Close second: **Education vs. conversion.** The MoR category isn't fully understood. A significant part of the marketing work is teaching the market — explaining the problem before the solution. But we also need pipeline now.

### What Winning Looks Like (2–3 Years)

- **Category ownership:** Recognized as the leading Merchant of Record for Africa — understood, not just known
- **Clear customer association:** Default partner for companies expanding into Africa and African companies expanding across markets
- **Predictable growth system:** Repeatable acquisition channels, not one-off campaigns
- **Deeper customer relationships:** More customers using Startbutton as core infrastructure, not a temporary solution
- **Trust as the differentiator:** The safe, credible choice for high-stakes expansion decisions

## My Expertise and Lens

### What I Know That Most People Don't

The biggest barrier to expansion in Africa isn't infrastructure — **it's coordination across fragmented systems.** Companies don't fail because one thing is hard. They fail because payments, regulation, tax, and local nuance are all moderately hard at the same time. Imported playbooks miss this entirely. You have to understand the interaction, not just the individual parts.

### What Analysis I Value vs. Dismiss

**I value:**
- Analysis that connects market reality to execution
- Explanations of *why* things work or don't work in practice
- Breakdowns grounded in real constraints (team size, resources, behavior)
- Clear signal vs. noise distinction

**I immediately dismiss:**
- Generic growth advice
- High-level frameworks with no application
- Playbooks imported from other markets that ignore African context
- Analysis that sounds smart but has no path to use

If I can't see how to use it, it's not useful.

### Contrarian Beliefs

**Narrative and trust matter as much as — or more than — product features.**

Most fintech infrastructure people believe: if the product works, it will sell. I don't think that's true in this market. The product is complex, the stakes are high, the market is fragmented. Decisions are driven by who is trusted, who is understood, who feels credible.

**Distribution and positioning are systematically undervalued relative to product in African tech.**

Solid products don't get adopted at the level they should — not because the product is weak, but because the story isn't clear, the market isn't educated, and the right people aren't being reached. This is the central problem worth solving.
