# OPay: research brief

*Questing own piece · Research v1, 20 Oct 2026 · Public record only*

## The single lesson (working)

> In a low-trust market, trust isn't built with brand. It's built in two boring layers: the money always lands, and the money grows while it sits.

Layer 1, **reliability**, gets people to deposit. Layer 2, **incentives**, gets them to stay. The research below tests both.

---

## Layer 1: the money always lands

**What's established**
- **The 2023 naira redesign broke the banks' digital rails.** Electronic payments fell from ₦39.6trn in Jan 2023 to ₦37.6trn in Feb 2023. By March 2023 only 60% of failed e-payment transactions had been resolved. [Ecofin, Sep 2025]
- **OPay and PalmPay became the default alternative.** By 24 March 2023 they ranked #1 and #2 among Nigeria's most-downloaded finance apps on Google Play (SimilarWeb), ahead of every bank app. [Semafor, 27 Mar 2023] By Oct 2023, OPay was Nigeria's most-downloaded app in any category. [TechCabal, Aug 2025]
- **OPay's own explanation is preparation, plus closed-loop transfers.** PR head Femi Hansen: "We have significantly invested in our payment infrastructure to ensure our system can withstand a cashless environment." The sharpest rise was in **OPay-to-OPay transfers, which never touch another bank.** He also admitted "most transactions have to go through other players" with weaker infrastructure. [TechCabal, 13 Mar 2023]
- **Bank failures kept repeating after 2023.** A major bank's core-system migration in late 2024 caused multi-day downtime and delayed salaries by up to 72 hours. [Ecofin, Sep 2025]
- **Scale today:** about 10m daily active users and about 100m daily transactions (2024); over 500k agents (2023), roughly 37% of Nigeria's banking agents by mid-2023. [TechCabal, Aug 2025; Ecofin, Sep 2025]

**Claims to treat carefully**
- "Near-100% uptime" during the crunch is **OPay's own claim**, not independently verified. [Ecofin]
- Market-share figures (60–70% of mobile money by value; 30–40% of POS count) are **unsourced estimates**. Use NIBSS or CBN data or drop them.
- The only hard success-rate number in the record is **PalmPay's 99.5%** (company claim, May 2025). There's no published OPay equivalent.

**The angle the record suggests:** the closed loop. When both sender and receiver are on OPay, the bank rails never get involved. Every new user makes the network more reliable for everyone already on it. That's reliability as a network effect, not just good engineering.

## Layer 2: the money grows while it sits

**What's established**
- **OWealth** is OPay's savings product, paying daily interest. [Condia partner piece, Aug 2026]
- **The 2026 "7 Savings Festival"** (10 Aug–27 Sep): **27% p.a.** on Target Savings of ₦77k–₦777k if not withdrawn early, plus a ₦77m reward pool. ⚠️ This comes from a **paid partner article**, i.e. OPay's own messaging. [Condia; MSME Africa]
- **Rewards have been part of the playbook from the start.** In 2023 PalmPay described its strategy as "exciting discounts, freebies, cash-back rewards, and earning opportunities", and OPay competed the same way. [Semafor, Mar 2023]

**Gaps**
- No public data on **how many users hold OWealth balances**, total deposits, or how the standard daily rate has moved against CBN's policy rate and inflation over time.
- So the claim that "interest is why they stay" isn't proven yet. That's the open question below.

---

## Friction to include (keeps the piece honest)

- **29 Apr 2024:** CBN ordered OPay, PalmPay, Kuda and Moniepoint to stop onboarding new customers "pending further notice", over accounts allegedly used for illicit FX. The fintechs said about 90% of implicated accounts were at commercial banks. [Nairametrics] *(Need to confirm when it was lifted.)*
- **2024:** OPay was fined **₦1bn** after a compliance audit. [TechCabal, Aug 2025]
- **March 2023:** viral reports of a CBN suspension, which CBN denied. [Semafor]

This is the tension: the trust users give OPay isn't fully shared by the regulator. Reliability won users, and compliance is the cost of keeping them.

1. When did *you* first trust OPay with money that mattered, and what happened?
2. Have you seen someone pick OPay over their own bank at the point of payment (a POS, a market, a filling station)? Tell me the moment.
